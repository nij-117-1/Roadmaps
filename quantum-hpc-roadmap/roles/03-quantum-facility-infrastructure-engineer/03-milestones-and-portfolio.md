# Role 3: Milestones and Portfolio

## Milestones (self-check)
- [ ] Explain the purpose of each cryostat temperature stage and what heat sources affect it
- [ ] Describe why attenuators are placed at several stages of the microwave line
- [ ] Draft a site-survey checklist covering magnetic fields, vibration, temperature, humidity, sound, and floor loading
- [ ] Estimate the power and cooling needs of a 20-qubit system from published specifications, and compare with a GPU node
- [ ] Explain why environmental drift causes calibration drift
- [ ] Write an alarm and escalation table for a cryostat and a cooling-water loop
- [ ] Describe the safety measures for a helium or nitrogen leak in a closed room

## Portfolio projects
1. **Site-readiness assessment:** a comparison of two candidate rooms for a quantum system, scored on power, cooling, floor, EMI, and access. Use the LRZ paper as a template.
2. **Facility monitoring dashboard:** Grafana panels for room temperature, humidity, water flow, and mock cryostat temperatures, with alert thresholds.
3. **Commissioning checklist:** a step-by-step acceptance test for a new system, from utility checks through to a first calibration run.
4. **Operations runbook:** procedures for a cooling-water fault, a power interruption, and a planned cooldown.
5. **Power and cooling model:** a spreadsheet or small script that estimates site load and redundancy for a mixed GPU and QPU deployment.

## Interview preparation
- **Site design:** you are asked to plan a room for a 20-qubit superconducting system next to a GPU cluster. What do you check first, and why?
- **Incident response:** the cryostat temperature rises overnight. Walk through the first 30 minutes.
- **Trade-offs:** when would you choose a pulse-tube cryostat over a helium-based system, and what are the operational consequences?
- **Physics basics:** explain why the qubit needs millikelvin temperatures and how that shapes the facility design.
