# Role 3: Materials

## Free: integration and facility case studies
- arXiv:2509.12949, "First Practical Experiences Integrating Quantum Computers with HPC Resources" (LRZ and IQM). Read the site survey, power, cooling, and operations sections closely.
- SC25 Workshops version of the same paper (ACM Digital Library)

## Free: cryogenics and wiring
- Krinner et al., "Engineering cryogenic setups for 100-qubit scale superconducting circuit systems", EPJ Quantum Technology, 2019 (free on arXiv)
- Krantz et al., "A Quantum Engineer's Guide to Superconducting Qubits", Applied Physics Reviews, 2019 (free on arXiv)
- Vendor documentation on cryostat operation (where publicly available)

## Free: quantum basics for facility staff
- IBM Quantum Learning: *Use a Quantum Computer Today* (hardware and calibration from the user side)
- IBM Quantum Learning: *Quantum Computing in Practice* (noise and mapping)
- MIT OCW 8.04 (Zwiebach) lectures 1–5, for the qubit basics

## Textbooks
- Pobell, *Matter and Methods at Low Temperatures* (Springer, 3rd ed.). Standard cryogenics reference.
- Any standard reference on cryogenic engineering and vacuum technology (choose one with a chapter on dilution refrigerators)

## Industry standards and guidance (check availability)
- Your site's existing standards for cryogen safety, oxygen monitoring, and electrical safety
- Standards for data-centre power and cooling (for example, ASHRAE guidelines, which you may already use)

## Practical training
- Vendor site-acceptance and operator training (IQM, Quantum Machines, and others offer customer training; ask during procurement)
- University or national-lab cryogenics courses (search for short courses on dilution refrigerators)

## Paid / certification
- No single widely recognised facility-specific quantum certification exists yet. Use your existing data-centre or facilities credentials, and vendor-specific training where offered.
